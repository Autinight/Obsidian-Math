### Definition

> [!definition] Matrix Lie group
> A **matrix Lie group** is a Lie group realized as a subgroup
> $$
> G\subset GL(n,\mathbb F),
> \qquad \mathbb F=\mathbb R \text{ or } \mathbb C,
> $$
> whose group operation is ordinary matrix multiplication.

> [!note] Ambient matrix space
> Since
> $$
> G\subset GL(n,\mathbb F)\subset M_n(\mathbb F),
> $$
> tangent vectors to $G$ can be represented by matrices.

> [!proposition] Matrix exponential characterization of the Lie algebra
>
> $\mathfrak{g}$ can be characterized by
> $$
> \mathfrak g
> =
> \{X\in M_n(\mathbb F): e^{tX}\in G \text{ for all } t\in\mathbb R\}.
> $$
> Here
> $$
> e^{tX}
> =
> \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^kX^k}{k!}
> $$
> is the ordinary matrix exponential.
>


> [!proof] Sketch
> If $X\in\mathfrak g=T_IG$, then the exponential curve
> $$
> \gamma_X(t)=e^{tX}
> $$
> is the one-parameter subgroup generated by $X$, so
> $$
> \gamma_X(t)\in G
> $$
> for all $t$, and
> $$
> \gamma_X'(0)=X.
> $$
>
> Conversely, if
> $$
> e^{tX}\in G
> $$
> for all sufficiently small $t$, then the curve
> $$
> t\mapsto e^{tX}
> $$
> is a smooth curve in $G$ passing through $I$, and its derivative at $0$ is
> $$
> \frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}e^{tX}=X.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> X\in T_IG=\mathfrak g.
> $$




> [!property] Tangent space by left translation
> For every $g\in G$,
> $$
> T_gG=(dL_g)_I(\mathfrak g)=g\mathfrak g.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> V\in T_gG
> \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
> g^{-1}V\in \mathfrak g.
> $$

> [!proof]- Proof
> Since left multiplication
> $$
> L_g:G\to G,\qquad h\mapsto gh
> $$
> is a diffeomorphism with $L_g(I)=g$, its differential at $I$ is a linear isomorphism
> $$
> (dL_g)_I:T_IG\longrightarrow T_gG.
> $$
> By definition, $T_IG=\mathfrak g$, hence
> $$
> T_gG=(dL_g)_I(\mathfrak g).
> $$
>
> In the matrix Lie group case, if $X\in\mathfrak g$ and $\gamma(t)$ is a curve in $G$ with
> $$
> \gamma(0)=I,\qquad \gamma'(0)=X,
> $$
> then
> $$
> (dL_g)_I(X)
> =
> \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}g\gamma(t)
> =
> g\gamma'(0)
> =
> gX.
> $$
> Therefore
> $$
> (dL_g)_I(\mathfrak g)=g\mathfrak g,
> $$
> and so
> $$
> T_gG=g\mathfrak g.
> $$
>
> Finally, for $V\in T_gG$,
> $$
> V\in T_gG
> \Longleftrightarrow
> V=gX\text{ for some }X\in\mathfrak g
> \Longleftrightarrow
> g^{-1}V=X\in\mathfrak g.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> V\in T_gG
> \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
> g^{-1}V\in\mathfrak g.
> $$


> [!property] Left-invariant vector fields
> A left-invariant vector field determined by $A\in\mathfrak g$ is
> $$
> X_A(g)=gA.
> $$
> Thus left-invariant vector fields on $G$ are naturally identified with elements of $\mathfrak g$.

> [!definition] Maurer-Cartan form
> The left Maurer-Cartan form is
> $$
> \theta_g:T_gG\to\mathfrak g,
> \qquad
> \theta_g(V)=g^{-1}V.
> $$

> [!property] Matrix notation for the Maurer-Cartan form
> In matrix notation,
> $$
> \boxed{\theta=g^{-1}dg}
> $$
> where $dg$ is the matrix-valued $1$-form satisfying
> $$
> (dg)_g(V)=V.
> $$

> [!intuition] Intuition
> A matrix Lie group is a smooth group of invertible matrices.
> Its tangent vectors are matrix velocities, and the Maurer-Cartan form translates each velocity back to the identity.
