> [!proposition] Lie Algebra via One-Parameter Subgroups
> Let $G$ be a Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g=T_eG$. Then there is a natural bijection
> $$
> \mathfrak g
> \cong
> \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{LieGrp}}(\mathbb R,G),
> $$
> given by
> $$
> X\longmapsto \gamma_X,\qquad
> \gamma_X(t)=\exp(tX).
> $$
> The inverse map is
> $$
> \gamma\longmapsto \gamma'(0).
> $$

> [!proof]-
> For each $X\in\mathfrak g$, the curve
> $$
> \gamma_X(t)=\exp(tX)
> $$
> is a smooth homomorphism $\mathbb R\to G$, hence a one-parameter subgroup.
>
> Conversely, let $\gamma:\mathbb R\to G$ be a one-parameter subgroup and set
> $$
> X=\gamma'(0)\in T_eG=\mathfrak g.
> $$
> Since $\gamma$ is a homomorphism, it is the integral curve of the left-invariant vector field determined by $X$. By uniqueness of integral curves,
> $$
> \gamma(t)=\exp(tX).
> $$
> Thus every one-parameter subgroup is uniquely generated by its tangent vector at the identity.

> [!remark] Core Idea
> A Lie algebra element is the **infinitesimal generator** of a one-parameter subgroup:
> $$
> X
> \leftrightarrow
> \bigl(t\mapsto \exp(tX)\bigr).
> $$
> Therefore $\mathfrak g$ can be understood as the space of all infinitesimal one-parameter motions in $G$.
